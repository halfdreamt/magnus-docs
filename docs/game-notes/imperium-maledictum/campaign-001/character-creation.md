# Character creation guide

The next conversation should start from who Ryan wants to play, not from a list
of setting nouns. Useful opening prompts are: “What do you want to be good at?”,
“How do you prefer to solve trouble?”, and “What kind of compromised authority
sounds fun to serve?”

## Before the character

The core rules create the party's Patron first because the Patron determines
the missions, authority, resources, and tone. Campaign 001 does not need every
Patron statistic before discussing a protagonist, but it should establish at
least:

- Faction and Duty;
- what the Patron asks agents to do;
- why the Patron recruited this character;
- one helpful Boon;
- the Patron's broad Motivation and Demeanour.

Liabilities are selected and tracked secretly by the GM. The Patron should be a
usable, tolerable ally who wants the agents to succeed, even if their methods or
demands create pressure.

Campaign 001 now has that foundation. Read [patron.md](patron.md): the Patron is
an Invictus Acquisitor with a technoarcheological discipline, an Information
Motivation, and a Pragmatic Demeanour. Additional Boons and presentation details
remain open.

## Current protagonist direction

Ryan wants to play a very junior Tech-Adept whose sanctioned curiosity is a
source of joy. The character wants to discover and reclaim technology for the
Imperium and Cult Mechanicus and has a defining affinity with the noosphere.
This is a desired fantasy, not a completed mechanical build.

The strongest starting path to examine first is:

- Adeptus Mechanicus Faction at Augmented Grade I, preserving the feeling of a
  novice with substantial room to ascend;
- the Apprentice Explorator **Duty package** (a pre-allocated spend of Grade
  I's own bonuses, not an extra grant) as the initial benchmark because it
  emphasises Intelligence, Perception, Logic, Lore, Tech, and archeotech
  recovery;
- Lexmechanic as the investigation-and-data Role or Transmechanic as the
  communication, discipline, and machine-communion Role;
- a Noospheric data-tether bestowed during an opening prelude, with the exact
  acquisition path agreed openly. Note it is Exotic (never in market stock),
  so the realistic route is through the Patron, and it occupies one slot of
  the Grade I augmetic capacity (Toughness Bonus), two slots of which are
  already spent at creation.

Worked pitches, patron-package proposal, and an initial-path proposal are in
[character-options.md](character-options.md), pending Ryan's review. Compare
Lexmechanic and Transmechanic in play terms before applying their benefits,
then ensure the chosen build has Discipline (Composure), Tech (Security), and
investigative competence sufficient to make noospheric interaction a strength
with meaningful risk rather than an automatic solution. Letting the Patron
choose the Role after Ryan picks the concept is worth +50 XP (Player's Guide
p49) and is diegetically clean.

## Eight core steps

1. Characteristics: generate the nine base ratings—Weapon Skill, Ballistic
   Skill, Strength, Toughness, Agility, Intelligence, Perception, Willpower,
   and Fellowship. Point allocation is the most controlled option; random
   generation can award starting XP.
2. Origin: choose where the character was shaped—Agri World, Feudal World,
   Feral World, Forge World, Hive World, Shrine World, Schola Progenium, or
   Voidborn. Origin modifies Characteristics and grants a small item.
3. Faction: choose the institution or underworld in which the character served
   before recruitment. It grants Characteristics, a Duty, Skills, Talents,
   equipment, and starting personal Influence.
4. Role: choose how the character contributes to the Patron's team.
5. Equipment: collect the packages granted by Origin, Faction, and Role.
6. Adding detail: name, appearance, history, short-term goal, long-term goal,
   and connections.
7. Bringing the character to life: faith, fears, loyalties, secrets, opinion of
   the Patron, and lines they will or will not cross.
8. Finishing touches: Maximum Wounds, Critical Wound limit, Initiative, Fate,
   handedness, and optional Divination.

## Roles in plain language

| Role | What it feels like in play |
| --- | --- |
| Interlocutor | Negotiator, interrogator, deceiver, or social operator |
| Mystic | Psyker; powerful, feared, and exposed to the Warp's risks |
| Savant | Investigator, scholar, medic, technician, or analyst |
| Penumbra | Scout, infiltrator, thief, sniper, or covert operative |
| Warrior | Direct combat specialist with broad weapons training |
| Zealot | Relentless believer who prevails through conviction and sacrifice |

Role does not lock the character into one personality or career. Origin,
Faction, Role, Skills, and Talents can be combined across institutional lines;
the interesting question is why the Patron saw value in that combination.

## Beginner-friendly procedure for Campaign 001

1. Offer three short concept pitches based on Ryan's desired playstyle.
2. Explain only the factions and roles relevant to those pitches.
3. Agree on guided or random generation before rolling anything.
4. Build Characteristics and derived values openly, showing the arithmetic.
5. Record every mechanical choice and its source route.
6. End with a two-sentence identity: what the character does, what they want,
   and what makes service to the Patron personally dangerous.
7. Check that the campaign supplies medicine, investigation, social access,
   and combat support that a solo protagonist does not personally cover.
