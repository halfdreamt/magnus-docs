# Rules and setting primer

This is a play aid, not a substitute for the rulebook. Verify edge cases in the
private corpus using the routes in the game-level README.

## The premise in one minute

Humanity survives in the 41st Millennium inside the Imperium: an immense,
ancient, authoritarian empire held together by faith, bureaucracy, military
force, and sacrifice. Its institutions are powerful but mutually suspicious,
slow, and decaying. Technology is maintained as religion. Interstellar travel
and communication depend on the Warp, a hostile psychic realm that also makes
daemons, corruption, and psykers possible.

Campaign 001 is set by default in the Macharian Sector, far west of Terra. The
sector was conquered by the celebrated general Saint Macharius. It recently
emerged from the Noctis Aeterna, a period of isolation and catastrophe caused
by the opening of the Great Rift. Worlds are damaged, tithes and communications
have failed, old enemies have resurfaced, and every power bloc sees both danger
and opportunity.

The player characters are unusually capable but still human agents recruited
from obscurity by a powerful Patron. The Patron supplies authority, resources,
missions, and complications. The characters are not Space Marines and do not
command the setting; their leverage comes from preparation, expertise,
relationships, and knowing when to invoke their Patron's name.

## Major Imperial factions

- Adeptus Astra Telepathica: controls, trains, transports, and sacrifices
  psykers; provides psychic communication.
- Adeptus Mechanicus: the Machine Cult; controls technical knowledge,
  manufacture, and sacred maintenance.
- Adeptus Administratum: the colossal civil bureaucracy, including tithes,
  records, and logistics.
- Astra Militarum: the Imperial Guard, an enormous human army built on massed
  firepower and attrition.
- Adeptus Ministorum: the Ecclesiarchy, which enforces worship of the
  God-Emperor; includes militant religious orders.
- Inquisition: secretive investigators with extraordinary authority who hunt
  heresy, daemons, and xenos threats.
- Imperial Fleet: naval power, voidships, transport, and security in space.
- Rogue Trader Dynasties: hereditary explorers and merchants licensed to act
  beyond ordinary Imperial limits.
- Infractionists: criminals, smugglers, gangs, and illicit networks rather than
  one unified institution.

These factions can cooperate against a common enemy while simultaneously
fighting over jurisdiction, resources, secrets, and doctrine. A good mission
usually places the agents between two or three incompatible agendas.

## Basic test

1. The GM names a Skill or Characteristic and a Difficulty.
2. Difficulty modifies the target number. Challenging is +0; the full scale is
   Very Easy +60, Easy +40, Routine +20, Challenging +0, Difficult -10, Hard
   -20, and Very Hard -30.
3. Roll d100. A result equal to or below the modified target succeeds.
4. When degree matters, calculate Success Level (SL): target's tens digit minus
   roll's tens digit. Positive is better. The rules distinguish marginal
   success at +0 from marginal failure at -0.
5. A roll of 01–05 always succeeds and 96–00 always fails. A 99 is a Fumble.

Do not roll when an action is routine and failure would add nothing. Tests are
for uncertainty with meaningful consequences. Failed Tests should change the
situation rather than invite identical rerolls.

### Common variants

- Common Test: one character rolls against the task.
- Opposed Test: both sides roll; higher SL wins. A failed roll can win if the
  opponent does even worse.
- Extended Test: accumulate required SL across limited attempts or time.
- Advantage: after rolling, the player may reverse the two d100 digits if that
  helps.
- Disadvantage: reverse the digits if doing so makes the result worse.
- Multiple sources of Advantage and Disadvantage cancel pairwise; additional
  uncancelled instances after the first normally become +10 or -10.
- Assistance normally grants Advantage and requires the helper to have an
  Advance in the relevant Skill.

## Campaign-driving resources

### Influence

Influence ranges from -5 to +5 and measures standing with a faction or contact.
Applicable Influence adjusts a social Test by the same number of SL. Characters
can invoke their Patron's normally secret Influence by revealing their mandate,
but doing so can expose the Patron's involvement and change future
relationships. Influence is leverage, not mind control.

Record personal contacts separately from faction-wide Influence. Review gains
and losses during mission debriefs, based on who learned what the agents did.

### Superiority

Superiority is a shared measure of preparation and momentum against a specific
enemy. Researching the foe, scouting the battlefield, and creating a sound plan
can each grant it before combat, normally to a pre-combat maximum of +3. Once
per turn, a character may add the party's current Superiority to one Test's SL.

If Superiority meets or exceeds an NPC's Resolve, that NPC becomes Desperate;
they may flee, surrender, or make a last reckless effort. This makes
investigation useful in a fight even for a noncombat specialist. Superiority
can rise when important enemies fall and drop when the party suffers Critical
Wounds, defeat, or a major surprise.

### Fate

Player characters normally begin with 3 Fate. Spending a point grants a
short-term escape such as rerolling a failed Test, adding +1 SL, changing when
to act, suppressing Critical Wound effects briefly, or removing a Condition.
Spent Fate returns at the next session. Burning Fate is permanent and is
reserved for survival or similarly dire intervention.

## Combat in brief

- Initiative is Perception Bonus + Agility Bonus; highest acts first.
- The battlefield uses roughly defined Zones rather than exact grid squares.
- On a turn a character can Move and take one Action, in either order.
- A character normally has one Reaction before their next turn.
- Melee is an Opposed Melee Test; ranged fire is normally a Ranged Test, with a
  Dodge Reaction available in the circumstances allowed by the rules.
- A successful hit normally deals weapon Damage plus attack SL, reduced by
  Armour at the hit location. Remaining Damage becomes Wounds.
- Maximum Wounds = Strength Bonus + (2 × Toughness Bonus) + Willpower Bonus.
- At Maximum Wounds, a character is badly impaired and further damaging hits
  produce Critical Wounds. Untreated Critical Wounds exceeding Toughness Bonus
  put a character at immediate risk of death.
- Violence is deliberately dangerous. Cover, scouting, retreat, medicine, and
  nonviolent endings are expected tools rather than signs of failure.

## GM principles from the core game

- Give indispensable clues; use Tests to interpret them, gain extra context,
  or avoid costs. Each vital clue should have several routes of discovery.
- Let consequences and escalation redirect a mistaken investigation instead of
  halting play.
- Accept a player's stronger explanation when it fits the evidence and makes a
  better story.
- Make faction choices consequential. Most victories should please someone and
  inconvenience someone else.
- Reward caution and creative preparation with Superiority.
- Use Resolve to end encounters once the outcome is clear; enemies need not all
  fight to the death.
- Preserve small acts of courage, loyalty, or hope. Without some light, the
  setting's darkness becomes flat rather than tragic.
