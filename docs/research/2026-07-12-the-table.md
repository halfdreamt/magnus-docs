# The Table — the session toolkit and the gap loop

**Date:** 2026-07-12
**Status:** Design (Luna-authored on the night shift; Ryan's brief: review the project
as what it pivoted to — a tool for Ryan + Luna to DM games for human or LLM players;
ensure the in-session tools exist, and a sturdy system to document gaps and apply
them iteratively between sessions and campaigns.)
**Method:** a full rehearsal — Luna built her own exe and RAN a mock session end to
end with only the tools that exist ("The Dark of Caendale," seed 10: world shopping,
party creation, a tavern opening, an investigation, a skill check, a skirmish,
consequences as casts, an `advance`, session-2 prep) and ledgered every friction
point — plus the surface survey's lever-gap list.
**Related:** stage.md (the contract all of this extends), the-weave.md (S6 fed the
seat context), new-tenants.md (N2's sim combat feeds T3's table combat)

## What the rehearsal proved (and we must not rebuild)

`skim` finds the fun in one command. `scene` is a genuine read-aloud roster. The
spellbook's casts are the right abstraction and undo is cast-aware. The name-bridge
carries mechanical casts through an `advance` correctly. The seat context is rich
and disciplined — sixteen tick-correct neighbor stances and not one leaked secret.
The split between DM-grain facts and seat-grain context is correct everywhere;
every addition below stays on the right side of it.

## What it exposed

The sharpest finding is a defect, not a feature gap: **`advance` silently breaks
every id-based reference in the campaign.** Export ids shift across an advance (as
designed), the mechanical casts ride the name-bridge (as designed) — but the party
list, and every stage-only note/tag/link, are id-anchored and get neither remapped
nor re-attached. One advance and your party roster points at strangers; your hooks
evaporate. The between-sessions loop currently destroys session prep.

After that, the gaps are the atomic verbs of tabletop play: **there is no check**
(a PC attempting anything uncertain has no resolution primitive), **no table
combat** beyond a single 1v1 exchange with no writeback, **no rumor/knowledge
query** (investigation — a core session mode — has zero support; the town cannot
say what it knows), and **no gap ledger** (the dev loop's inbox doesn't exist —
friction goes nowhere).

## The waves

**T1 — The Surviving Campaign** (fix-class, first). `advance` remaps export-id
references — party, note/tag/link targets — through the same name/seed bridge the
casts already use, and re-attaches surviving stage-only edits to the new ids;
`create_entity` gets bridged or refused loudly at cast time (today a spawned being
blocks the advance later — fail at the right end). Plus the **change digest**:
an advance reports what happened while you were away — deaths, births, marriages,
raids, leadership turnover, route and stance shifts, scoped to the party and its
communities. Acceptance: the rehearsal's exact failure replayed green — party
intact, hooks intact, "the necromancer died in year 103" surfaced unprompted.

**T2 — The Session Verbs** (`check` · `sheet` · `gap`).
- `stage check <person> <attribute> [--dc N]` — a deterministic roll seeded on
  (campaign checksum, person, ordinal) so no seat can reroll it; modifier derived
  from calling + temperament (the combat-die idiom); the attempt appends as a cast
  — **checks are history**. A DC guidance table ships beside it.
- `stage sheet <person>` — the one-command player handout: person facts + calling
  + temperament + rank + kin + held Works + a derived combat rating.
- `stage gap "<what was missing>"` — `campaign.gaps[]`, timestamped against the
  present; `stage gaps report` aggregates across campaigns, and the spellbook's
  promotion report feeds it. **The loop's inbox: session → gap ledger → build →
  next session.** Cheap, and it prioritizes everything after it.

**T3 — The Skirmish.** Table combat over scene participants: initiative
(age-curve × trait), rounds depleting health through the existing combat-power
exchange, party-vs-group, **a Work in a combatant's custody multiplies their
power** (custody meets the table; N2's arms-and-scars work feeds straight in),
per-calling actions (the adept's art, the blade's press), and — the point —
**outcomes write back as wound/kill casts with named participants.** A table
fight becomes real history the next advance simulates around.

**T4 — The Town Knows** (`lore` + the town sheet). A belief-set derived from the
events a community witnessed or took part in, scoped by co-location and stance
(allies share rumors, hostiles distort them), graded known/rumored/unknown —
"who here knows about the tower?" finally has an answer. With it, the richer town
content the rehearsal had to invent by hand: a seeded town sheet (notable spots,
current mood from recent events, the flavor a scene opens with). Known honest
limit to carry: ambient dread keys off cascade events, which sealed worlds never
emit — the lore layer should read the doom ledger's slope directly.

**T5 — The Missing Casts.** The rehearsal-confirmed levers, added as codified
macros with real semantics + engine perturbation kinds, in play-demand order:
`raid` between named communities · `install`/`depose` a leader (a real
GovernanceTransition, not a rank number) · the `create_entity` bridge (spawn a
being that survives an advance) · `route` open/sever · `famine`/`relieve` ·
mechanical `marry` · lineage-wide `bless`/`curse` · `awaken` a latent bloodline ·
`plague` · `grant` a discovery. Each inherits the applier's fail-fast safety.

## Parity (the invariant, held throughout)

Anything a seat sees flows through `build_pc_context` (now-scoped, secret-filtered)
— never DM-grain facts. `check`: the player's *approach* is the Decision; the die
is engine-side, seat-independent. `skirmish`: each round a choice Decision over
legal actions, options from the scene, resolution after the choice. `lore`: pure
context enrichment through the existing gate. `sheet`/`gap`/digest: DM-facing, no
seat surface. LLM and heuristic players keep exact parity by construction.

## Order

T1 is fix-class and footprint-disjoint from the engine waves (tools/stage only) —
it can interleave with Push 3/4 engine slices. T2 unlocks a real session-zero.
T3–T4 are the session modes. T5 lands by demand, first two: the create_entity
bridge and `install`. Every wave: the playbook's verification discipline, plus a
rehearsal re-run of the exact scene that exposed the gap.
