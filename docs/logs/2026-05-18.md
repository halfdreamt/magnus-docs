- Magnus3: Reviewed the labor+gathering rewrite to reconstruct design intent (post-fix retrospective so I can pick up the next thread)
  - **The diagnosis that triggered the reset.** The old harvest system was a placeholder, not a model. Famine severity was computed, exported, and never read by any system — cosmetic. `drought_resilience` leader modifier was dead code. Food stores floored at 0, so multi-year granaries were impossible. The Tract/Plot/Area hierarchy I'd built that same morning (April 12) was decorative for yield — harvest read a flat regional `soil_fertility` and ignored everything below it. "Grain" was hardcoded into struct names, baking in an unwanted assumption. None of the structure was load-bearing — the plumbing existed but didn't carry water. Patching each gap would have just pushed the inconsistencies around, so I reset the model.
  - **Core idea: one formula, two regimes.** `yield = min(labor × efficiency, tract_cap × area_mod × region_mod) × variance`. The `min()` is doing the conceptual work — labor-bound (small community on lots of land): `labor × efficiency` binds → yield scales linearly with population → growth. Land-bound (large community, fixed tract): `tract_cap × mods` binds → yield plateaus → **carrying capacity emerges from geography**. Before, carrying capacity was a side-effect of a global `base_yield` constant — one number tunes the whole world. After, carrying capacity is local: a Forest in a Woodland biome supports ~31 people, a Bog in Highland ~9. Move a community, settle a new one, change the biome — population dynamics shift automatically. The Tract/Plot hierarchy stopped being decorative.
  - **Why per-season instead of per-year.** Matches the labor relationship (people gather and eat continuously, the autumn-only framing was a harvest artifact). And it sets up mid-year dynamics later — a future drought in summer can degrade gathering for one season, recover the next, which is hard to express without per-season cadence.
  - **Why delete drought / famine / weather in v1.** They were demonstrably broken or cosmetic. Reintroducing them on top of the new base would drag broken behavior forward or force a refactor the day after the rewrite. Cleaner to delete, get the base working, then reintroduce drought/famine as labor-disrupting events in the new vocabulary — not yield-multiplier patches over a system that no longer thinks in those terms.
  - **The April 13 follow-up: closing the food→population loop.** Once gathering was running, the obvious next gap appeared — food could go arbitrarily negative and no one died. Mortality model added the missing back-pressure that makes the equilibrium actually equilibrate: equal per-capita allocation each season; partial → person Starving, gates births; zero → per-person death roll (default 50%); Famine event opens while anyone starves, closes when nobody does. **Negative feedback closes the loop** — population overshoots capacity → some starve → births pause and some die → population settles back. Pre-rewrite the loop was open (food shortage just suppressed births via a hard threshold), so populations could collapse from stochastic noise; post-rewrite the loop tightens itself.
  - **The same-evening aggregation refactor.** Mortality worked but emitted 130k per-person Starvation events per 1000y, violating the principle "events represent exceptions, not routine state." At carrying capacity, mild seasonal starvation IS the routine. Folded the per-person events into the Famine event itself — one Famine per community per shortage spell, with a `vector<StarvationInterval>{person_id, start_tick, end_tick}` inside. Same data, 14× fewer events, and the structure now matches the narrative ("this community had a famine; here's who suffered in it").
  - **The throughline.** Every step takes a piece of *cosmetic* structure and makes it *load-bearing*: Tract/Plot existed → made them the food source; Famine events existed → made them kill people; spatial layout existed → made it determine carrying capacity; per-person events were emitted → folded the background ones into the foreground event. Pattern: "if the simulation generates a thing, the thing should affect the simulation." The rewrite was a sweep through the food system enforcing that.
  - **Where the perf regression came from, in this framing.** When I needed per-person starvation state, I reached for the natural ECS expression: `Starving` as a tag-like component, present-iff-currently-starving. That's the correct *meaning* — it's how it ought to read in inspection tools — but the *implementation* hit Flecs's archetype-table-move cost at a frequency the engine didn't budget for. Today's fix preserves the intent (state still in the ECS, still queryable, still per-entity) by changing only the representation: always-present component with a sentinel idle value. Same conceptual model, just stops triggering archetype churn. The regression wasn't a design mistake; it was an idiomatic implementation that didn't survive contact with this engine at this scale.
  - **Open threads parked at the bottom of the design doc, in roughly increasing scope:** labor age-weighting (exclude children/elders, weight by health), adjacent-tract foraging (gathering radius), per-biome food-source strategies (Gatherer/Farmer/Herder/Fisher), drought/famine reintroduction as labor-disrupting events, broader v2 of mortality (currently only zero-food kills). Drought reintroduction is probably the most natural next step since it slots into the labor formula directly.
