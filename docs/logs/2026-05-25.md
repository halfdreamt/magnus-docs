- Magnus3: First-pass framing — formalize per-mode parameters that plug into the existing equation
  - Equation today: `yield = min(labor × efficiency, tract_cap × (1 + leader_bonus)) × variance`
  - Proposed dial set so any "mode" is just a named configuration of dials: `efficiency`, `labor_curve` (linear/sub-linear/threshold), `tract_cap_fn` (how the mode reads tract attributes), `biome_affinity` (multiplier or hard gate), `tract_radius`, `depletion/recovery`, `variance_band`, `capital_state`, `cadence` (per-season / yearly cycle / seasonal peak), `leader_channel`
  - Walked five candidate modes (Gatherer / Hunter / Fisher / Pastoralist / Farmer) against the dials. Stress points that emerged: `tract_cap_fn` was doing a lot of work because three of five modes want the cap derived from a *different* tract attribute than the gatherable-food cap; `capital` and `depletion` are the two dials with no precedent in current code (both add persistent inter-season state); `cadence` is the one dial that genuinely breaks the equation's shape because Farmer's annual plant-then-harvest doesn't fit "compute every season."
- Magnus3: Ryan reframed the model — modes were the wrong abstraction
  - **The reframe:** communities don't *be* a mode; they *allocate labor* across whatever sources their land affords. Sources naturally form a 2×2 grid: **Flora/Fauna × Wild/Domesticated**. Hunting and fishing collapse into Fauna-Wild (gating differences become biome/sub-type variation, not separate modes). Gathering is Flora-Wild. Farming is Flora-Domesticated. Herding is Fauna-Domesticated.
  - **The deeper move** was the layer separation: properties of the *sources themselves* (intrinsic to land) vs. capacity of the *community and its members* to process those sources (intrinsic to the community). Each layer owns different state; the yield equation factors across both.
- Magnus3: Worked out the two-layer model in more detail
  - **Source layer (on the tract):** each tract carries a vector of sources, one per active cell of the 2×2 it can support. Per source: `potential` (ceiling the land could support), `standing_stock` (current state — game density, established cropland, available grazing; wild stocks replenish naturally, domesticated stocks need labor to exist), `biome_multiplier`. A coastal forest might have all four cells active; a bog might have only Flora-Wild; tundra only Fauna-Wild. The land *affords* a configuration; it doesn't dictate behavior.
  - **Community capability layer (on the community):** `labor_pool` (eventually age-weighted), `per-cell processing_capacity` (efficiency × skill × leader_bonus × tool_stock for each cell — a community can be good at hunting but poor at farming on land that supports both), `allocation_policy` (how labor splits across available sources each season), `capital_state` per domesticated cell (herd size, cleared/planted acreage — the tract holds *potential* cropland/grazing; the community holds *the actual herd / the actual field*).
  - **Per-source yield:** `yield_s = min(labor_s × efficiency_s, stock_s × harvest_rate_s) × variance_s`. Total community food = Σ yield_s. Same `min(labor, land)` shape as today, just per-source and summed.
- Magnus3: What this factoring unlocks
  - **Mixed economies emerge naturally** — no mode-switching, no either/or. A community fishes in spring, gathers in summer, harvests in autumn from the same allocator.
  - **Identical land, different communities** — two settlements on the same coastal forest can specialize differently based on accumulated capital and skill.
  - **Transitions are continuous** — adopting agriculture isn't a mode flip; it's the community starting to invest labor into Flora-Domesticated capital on a tract that has the potential. Wild→domesticated plays out as capital accumulation over generations.
  - **Tract value becomes legible** — a tract's "carrying capacity" stops being one number; it's a vector of source potentials. Migration and fission decisions can read which cells are unsaturated.
  - **Leader bonuses sharpen** — instead of one `gathering_bonus`, a leader has affinities per cell.
- Magnus3: Open design questions surfaced, parked for the next session
  - **Capital ownership on transitions:** herds and fields are *of* the community but *on* the tract. Leaning toward community-owns-capital + references-tract, so fission/migration forces a deliberate choice about what to take, leave, or rebuild.
  - **Sub-types within a cell:** Fauna-Wild bundles hunting and fishing; mechanically large game / small game / fish behave differently. First-class entries in the source vector vs. biome-modifier variation within one Fauna-Wild entry — undecided.
  - **Allocation policy is its own design surface.** Proportional-to-expected-return is fine for v1; cultural inertia and risk preference get interesting later. Budgeting note: this is per-community per-season — same hot path that the archetype-churn fix just rescued — so it has to stay cheap arithmetic.
  - **Wild ↔ Domesticated binary vs. continuum:** voting binary for v1. The gradual story emerges from capital accumulating in domesticated cells over generations; we don't need a continuous coordinate.
  - **What replaces the "default mode" today:** every community starts with allocation toward the wild cells the land affords (Domesticated cells start at zero capital, inert until labor is invested). Domestication emerges from sustained investment rather than being chosen at founding.
- Magnus3: Where to push next — the load-bearing threads are allocation policy and capital-on-fission. The broader open question is whether to step further back first and reconsider how this changes the existing Tract/Plot data model and the Famine event shape before going deeper on any single piece.
